# 0% Furniture Financing in the Dallas–Fort Worth Metroplex

**Research date:** September 2026
**Context:** Sourcing furniture for room/house rentals — evaluating who in DFW offers genuine interest-free terms and what the traps are.

> **Promotional terms change constantly.** Every figure here is what the retailer or a
> reputable secondary source advertised at research time. Confirm the exact terms sheet
> *in writing at the register* before signing. Financing offers are almost always
> "subject to credit approval" and frequently run in short windows.

---

## 1. The single most important distinction: deferred interest vs. true 0% APR

Most "0% financing" in furniture retail is **not** 0% APR. It is **deferred interest**.
The two are structurally different products and one of them is a trap.

| | True 0% APR | Deferred interest |
|---|---|---|
| Advertised as | "0% APR for 24 months", "equal monthly payments, no interest" | "**No interest if paid in full** within 24 months" |
| Interest during promo | Never accrues | Accrues silently from **purchase date** |
| If you miss the deadline | You pay interest only on the remaining balance, going forward | **The entire accrued interest bucket lands on your statement** — retroactive to day one, on the original purchase amount |
| Payment sizing | Payments are the balance ÷ months, so paying the minimum retires the balance on time | Minimum payments are **not** sized to clear the balance in the window |

Key facts:

- The **language test** is reliable: *"0% intro APR for N months"* = true 0%.
  *"No interest if paid in full within N months"* = deferred interest.
- Roughly **one in five deferred-interest promotional balances** ends up getting hit with
  retroactive interest.
- The CFPB has [publicly pressed retail card issuers](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/consumer-financial-protection-bureau-encourages-retail-credit-card-companies-consider-more-transparent-promotions/)
  to make these offers more transparent, citing exactly this surprise-charge problem.
- Bob's Discount Furniture drew a TINA.org complaint and subsequent state-official scrutiny
  over minimum payments that fall short of clearing the promo balance in time.

**The back-interest rates are brutal**, which is what makes the miss so expensive:

| Card | Purchase APR after promo |
|---|---|
| Living Spaces (Synchrony) | **34.99%** purchase / **39.99%** penalty (new accounts as of 7/31/25) |
| IKEA Projekt | 21.99% |
| Nebraska Furniture Mart | ~18% (accrues from delivery date) |

A $4,000 furnishing package on a 34.99% card, missed by one month at 24 months, can add
well over $1,500 in a single statement.

### The operational fix

If you take a deferred-interest offer, treat it as a hard obligation:

1. Divide the balance by **(promo months − 1)**, not by the promo months. Pay that fixed
   amount. The spare month absorbs a billing-cycle surprise.
2. Set autopay for that fixed amount, **not** the statement minimum.
3. Calendar the payoff deadline independently of the card's own reminders.
4. Watch out for returns/credits — a refund can reallocate payments across plans and
   quietly leave a few dollars sitting on the promo balance. A $3 remainder detonates
   the whole interest bucket.
5. Never put a new non-promo purchase on the same card. Payment allocation rules will
   generally send your money to the highest-APR balance, not the one with the deadline.

## 2. Tier 1 — National / big-box chains with real DFW locations

### Havertys — *best structure in the market*

**The only DFW retailer found offering genuine 0% APR (not deferred interest) on long terms.**

| Offer | Terms |
|---|---|
| **0% APR, 48 months** | Furniture $7,499+ / mattress $4,999+, 10% down |
| **0% APR, 36 months** | Purchases $3,999+, 10% down |
| No interest if paid in full, 18 or 24 mo | 10% down (deferred interest) |
| No interest if paid in full, 6 or 12 mo | **No minimum purchase, no down payment** (deferred interest) |

Card issued by Synchrony. The high minimums on the true-0% tiers are reachable if you're
furnishing multiple rooms in one transaction — and at that size, the structural
difference is worth engineering the purchase around.

**DFW locations:** Dallas (4505 Lyndon B Johnson Fwy, 75244), Southlake (200 N Kimball Ave,
76092), plus Plano and Frisco.

### Nebraska Furniture Mart — *best for volume + immediate availability*

The Colony store is 560,000 sq ft — the largest home furnishings store in North America —
sitting on top of a distribution center holding ~500,000 pieces, which means **same-day
take-home on much of the catalog**. For furnishing several rooms on a schedule, that
logistics advantage is often worth more than the financing terms.

- NFM Card promotional plans run **6, 12, 18, 24, or 36 months** depending on category and spend.
- Furniture commonly sits at **18 months**.
- Mattresses: **24 months** with equal payments on $999+; **12 months** on $399+.
- 36-month plans have appeared on flooring at $1,999+.
- Structure: no interest if paid in full with equal payments — but **interest accrues at
  ~18% APR from date of delivery** if you don't clear it or if you miss a payment.

NFM's ~18% back rate is roughly *half* what the Synchrony store cards charge. If you're
going to carry deferred-interest risk anywhere, this is the cheapest place to carry it.

**Location:** 5600 Nebraska Furniture Mart Dr, The Colony, TX 75056 · (972) 668-3000

### Rooms To Go — *equal-payment structure, tiered by spend*

Rooms To Go's plans are **equal monthly payments** (balance ÷ months, rounded up), which
means paying the scheduled amount actually retires the balance in the window. That's a
meaningfully safer structure than minimum-payment deferred interest.

| Tier | Terms |
|---|---|
| Base | 6 months no interest |
| $1,000 – $1,499.99 | 9 months no interest |
| $1,500+ | 12 months no interest |
| $1,475+ (promo through 9/7/26) | **60 equal monthly payments** |
| **72 months interest-free**, in-store only | Min. **$2,499**, **20% down**; premium mattresses only — Tempur-Pedic, Stearns & Foster, Helix, Beautyrest Black |

Card issued by Synchrony. Multiple DFW-area stores including Fort Worth.

### Ashley Store

| Offer | Minimum |
|---|---|
| 6 months no interest | — |
| 12 months no interest | $499+ |
| 24 months no interest | $999+ |
| 60 equal monthly payments | $1,499+ (online) |

Ashley Advantage card, issued by Synchrony. The "no interest if paid in full" tiers are
**deferred interest**; the 60-month plan is equal-payment. Ashley has the densest DFW
store footprint of any Tier 1 name, and the $499 threshold for 12 months is the lowest
entry point among the big chains.

### Living Spaces — *highest back-interest risk on this list*

Synchrony deferred-interest promos ranging **12 to 48 months** depending on the campaign;
the 12-month 0% typically requires **$1,000+** on a single receipt. Qualifying purchase
must be on one receipt.

⚠️ **Purchase APR 34.99%, penalty APR 39.99%** for new accounts as of 7/31/25. This is the
most expensive miss on the list. Excellent stores; be disciplined with the card.

**DFW locations:** Fort Worth (8460 Tehama Ridge Pkwy, 76177), Grand Prairie (off SH-161),
plus Plano/Grapevine-area stores.

### Furniture Row — *lowest minimums, good for per-room buys*

Recent promo cycle (8/21/26 – 9/3/26) on the Furniture Row credit card:

- **12 months** no interest on **$350+**
- **24 months** no interest on **$700+**

Deferred interest ("interest charged from the purchase date if not paid in full"). Those
minimums are by far the lowest of any national chain here, which makes Furniture Row the
natural fit for furnishing **one room at a time** rather than a whole house at once.
Frisco location plus other DFW-area stores.

### IKEA — *best fine print of any card on this list*

IKEA Projekt Card:

- **6 months** on $500+
- **12 months** on $1,000+
- **24 months** on $2,500+
- APR after: 21.99%

The critical detail: IKEA's terms state interest is charged **"from the end of the
promotional period on any remaining promotional plan balance."** That is **waived
interest, not retroactive deferred interest** — missing the deadline costs you interest
going forward on what's left, not a retroactive bucket on the original amount. That is a
materially better failure mode than every Synchrony store card here.

Caveat: minimum payments are explicitly *not* guaranteed to clear the promo balance in
the window, so still size your own payments. You can prequalify without a hard pull.

**DFW locations:** Frisco and Grand Prairie.

### Mattress Firm

Partnered with **Affirm** for in-store and online pay-over-time, "as low as 0% APR", with
select 0% APR terms advertised at 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 years. Dense DFW footprint. See the
BNPL caveat in §5 — "as low as 0%" is rate-based on credit, not a flat offer.

## 5. ⚠️ Tier 3 — The "0%" that isn't: lease-to-own and BNPL

### Lease-to-own (Acima, Snap Finance, Progressive Leasing, Katapult, Koalafi)

These are marketed alongside financing and often labeled "no credit needed" or
"90 days same as cash." **They are not credit and they are not 0%.** They are leases.

- **Only the 90-day early purchase option** approaches cash price — and even then you're
  typically paying a fee on top (Acima: **$50 upfront**).
- Past 90 days, total cost of ownership generally runs **1.5× to 2.5× the cash price**.
- On 12-month plans specifically: **Progressive ≈ +20%** over cash, **Acima ≈ +30%**.
- Consumers who don't exercise the early buyout **routinely pay more than double retail**.
  This is the single most common complaint across Trustpilot, BBB and Reddit for these
  providers.
- Structural difference worth knowing: Progressive charges flat fees rather than interest;
  Acima's leases include interest.

**Regulatory context (relevant to your protections, not to the price):** the CFPB sued
Acima in July 2024 alleging deceptive dark patterns obscuring the true cost of its
agreements — then **voluntarily dismissed the case with prejudice in March 2025**.
Separately, a Utah federal judge dismissed the CFPB's case against Snap Finance, holding
the lease-to-own products **do not meet the statutory definition of credit**. The practical
takeaway: these products currently sit *outside* much of the consumer-credit protection
framework. That's a reason for more caution, not less.

**Verdict:** only rational if you will definitely exercise the 90-day buyout and you have
no other approval path. Otherwise it is the most expensive way to furnish a property in
this market.

### BNPL (Affirm, Klarna, Afterpay)

- **Affirm Pay-in-4 is genuinely 0% APR.** Available at Mattress Firm, Wayfair, West Elm.
- **Affirm's longer monthly plans are 0–36% APR based on credit.** "As low as 0% APR" is
  a rate range, not an offer — check your actual quoted APR before accepting.
- **Klarna + Article:** four interest-free payments, or longer financing on larger orders.
- **Wayfair Card:** 6–24 months deferred interest depending on spend ($199 to $2,999+
  thresholds); **12 months on $799+**. Separately, a Major Purchase Plan at **9.99% fixed**
  for 36/48/60 months on minimums of $1,599 / $1,799 / $1,999.

## 6. Notes for furnishing rental properties specifically

### Free float is the actual product

If you can pay cash, 0% financing is still worth taking — it's an interest-free working
capital loan that lets you furnish more units per dollar of deployed cash. The whole value
proposition, though, is conditional on never missing the payoff. One missed 24-month
deferred-interest deadline at 34.99% wipes out the float benefit of several successful ones.

### Always price the cash discount against the float

Independents will frequently discount for cash. **A 10–20% cash discount beats 6 months of
free float, and usually beats 12.** Note especially that some "90 days same as cash"
programs — Sam's Furniture is explicit about this — are priced off **everyday retail rather
than sale price**, meaning you silently forgo the promotional pricing in exchange for the
term. Always compute both totals.

### Logistics may matter more than terms

For multi-room furnishing on a turnover deadline, NFM's on-site distribution center
(~500,000 pieces, same-day take-home) can be worth more than a few extra months of
interest-free terms. A unit sitting vacant waiting on a 6-week special order costs real rent.

### Tax treatment (verify with your CPA)

- Furniture in a residential rental is **5-year MACRS personal property**, depreciated
  separately from the building's 27.5-year schedule.
- **De minimis safe harbor:** taxpayers without an applicable financial statement (most
  individual investors) may expense items up to **$2,500 per item or per invoice**. This
  covers most individual furniture pieces. It requires **filing the election with your
  return each year** and treating the amounts as expenses on your books.
  → Practical implication: **ask for itemized invoices**, not one lump receipt. A single
  $6,000 invoice may fail the per-invoice test that six $1,000 itemized invoices would pass.
- **Section 179** for 2026: up to **$2,560,000**, phasing out above **$4,090,000** of
  qualifying property placed in service. Note that §179 for rentals carries real
  restrictions — the activity generally must rise to a trade or business, and §179 cannot
  create a loss. Confirm applicability before relying on it.
- **Financing does not change the deduction.** You deduct or depreciate based on cost when
  placed in service, not on payments made. So 0% financing plus immediate expensing is a
  genuine, compounding cash-flow advantage: you get the full deduction in year one while
  paying the cost out over 12–48 months at no charge.

### Rental as an alternative to purchase

**CORT Furniture Rental** — 14215 Inwood Rd, Dallas — offers whole-home and single-room
packages with delivery and setup. Not a purchase and not 0% financing, but worth pricing
for short-hold properties, staging, or testing a furnished-unit thesis before committing
capital.

## 7. Recommended shortlist

| If you are… | Go to |
|---|---|
| Furnishing multiple rooms in one transaction ($4k+) | **Havertys** — the only true 0% APR (36 mo at $3,999+, 48 mo at $7,499+, 10% down). Structurally safest long-term money in the market. |
| Furnishing one room at a time | **Furniture Row** — 12 mo at $350+, 24 mo at $700+. Lowest minimums anywhere. |
| Needing inventory *this week* | **Nebraska Furniture Mart**, The Colony — same-day availability, 18 mo typical on furniture, and the lowest back-interest rate (~18%) if something goes wrong. |
| Worried about missing a payoff deadline | **IKEA Projekt** — waived interest, not retroactive. Best failure mode of any card here. Or **Havertys/Rooms To Go** equal-payment plans, where paying the schedule clears the balance by construction. |
| Wanting to negotiate on price | **Weir's**, **Home Zone**, **Furniture Nation** — independents; ask about cash discounts *and* current 0% campaigns, then compare totals. |
| Unable to get approved for a store card | **Furniture Nation layaway** first. Then a **90-day same-as-cash** program you are certain you can clear. Lease-to-own past 90 days should be the last resort. |
